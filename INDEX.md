# Desert Car Optimization

### A wheel-integrated hexapod concept for desert mobility

**Abu Dhabi University — College of Engineering**  
**MEC 482: Introduction to Mechatronics**

---

## Project overview

Our project investigates a desert vehicle concept that integrates a hexapod mechanism with a wheel. The goal is to explore whether this arrangement can improve the vehicle's mobility over loose or uneven desert terrain compared with a conventional wheel setup.

The mechanism, actuation, vehicle platform, and evaluation criteria are still being developed. Any performance benefit is a hypothesis and must be established through analysis and controlled testing.

**Project stage:** Concept development.  
**Primary comparison:** Hexapod-integrated wheel versus a conventional wheel under matched test conditions.  
**Project supervisor and course professor:** Dr. Claudio Vignola

## Research question

**Can a wheel-integrated hexapod mechanism improve a desert vehicle's ability to traverse loose or uneven terrain without unacceptable increases in energy use, complexity, or instability?**

Our working hypothesis is that changing how the wheel contacts and moves over the ground may improve mobility in selected terrain conditions. Testing will determine whether this is supported and identify trade-offs against a conventional wheel.

## Concept and engineering inspiration

A hexapod uses six coordinated legs or contact elements to support and move a system. In this project, the idea is to place or integrate a hexapod mechanism at a vehicle wheel. The precise geometry—whether the legs supplement the wheel, replace its ground-contact function, or operate in a selectable mode—needs to be defined in the design phase.

The project will document the selected mechanism and its movement before making claims about its expected performance. Relevant design considerations include ground contact, traction, obstacle clearance, load distribution, stability, actuation, and protection from sand and dust.

## Initial design questions

- What is the mechanism's geometry and how is it attached to the wheel or vehicle?
- How are the six legs actuated and coordinated?
- Can the vehicle switch between wheel and hexapod operation, if both modes are intended?
- What vehicle mass, payload, speed, terrain, and obstacle conditions should the design support?
- How will the mechanism be protected from sand, dust, and impacts?
- What safety limits and course approvals apply to prototype testing?

## System kinematics and dynamics

For wheel rotational speed \(N\) in revolutions per minute, angular speed is:

\[\omega = \frac{2\pi N}{60}\]

For an effective wheel radius \(r\), ideal ground speed without slip is:

\[v = r\omega\]

Actual speed may differ because of wheel slip, terrain deformation, or the motion of the hexapod mechanism. The team will define and measure the relevant variables once the prototype configuration is established.

Useful evaluation quantities may include drawbar pull, slip ratio, obstacle clearance, travel speed, energy consumption, and vehicle stability. The final set will depend on the project scope and available test equipment.

## Design optimization

The design will be evaluated against a conventional wheel baseline. Candidate criteria include:

| Criterion | How it may be evaluated |
|---|---|
| Mobility on loose terrain | Distance or ability to traverse a repeatable sand track |
| Traction | Pull force or wheel/vehicle slip under a defined load |
| Obstacle traversal | Successful traversal of obstacles with documented dimensions |
| Energy use | Electrical power or energy per distance, if instrumented |
| Stability | Tilt, rocking, or other documented stability measure |
| Complexity and mass | Mechanism mass, part count, and assembly requirements |
| Reliability | Repeated trials and recorded failures or maintenance |

No scores or performance results are assigned yet. Any decision matrix will be completed using documented design evidence or measured results, not assumed outcomes.

## Design and manufacturing

The team will develop the mechanism and vehicle integration in CAD after confirming the architecture, dimensions, loads, and available materials. Prototype manufacturing methods and components will be selected to suit the approved design and budget.

The design should account for secure attachment, moving-part clearances, structural strength, serviceability, and protection against sand ingress. Fabricated parts must be inspected before testing.

## Data collection and experimental validation

The hexapod-integrated wheel and conventional wheel should be tested under matched conditions wherever possible. The team will document the test surface, vehicle or test-rig mass, payload, battery condition, mechanism settings, and trial procedure.

| Measurement | Unit / record |
|---|---|
| Travel distance or test-track completion | m / pass-fail |
| Time to complete a test | s |
| Drawbar pull, if measured | N |
| Electrical voltage and current, if measured | V, A |
| Energy or power, if measured | Wh, W |
| Wheel or mechanism speed, if measured | RPM |
| Slip, if measured | % or stated method |
| Prototype mass | kg |
| Failures, adjustments, and observations | Written trial log |

Repeated trials will be used where practical. Results will include the test conditions, averages and variability when enough trials are available, and limitations. No test results are claimed until measurements have been collected.

## Safety and approval

- Keep hands, clothing, and tools clear of moving wheels, legs, linkages, gears, and actuators.
- Secure the vehicle or test rig and use an accessible stop or power-disconnect method during tests.
- Inspect fasteners, structural parts, wiring, and batteries before operation.
- Use suitable eye protection and a controlled test area; keep bystanders outside the test zone.
- Avoid uncontrolled vehicle operation and obtain instructor approval for the test plan.
- Take precautions for dust and sand, including protecting electronics and following component limits.
